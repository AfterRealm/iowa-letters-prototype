Iowa Letters

A small scholarly digital edition of letters home from Iowa volunteers serving in the American Civil War, built as a prototype demonstrating how a Libraries IT developer would partner with the Digital Scholarship and Publishing Studio to deliver a faculty-led digital edition project.

About this edition

Between 1861 and 1865 the State of Iowa raised forty-eight infantry regiments and sent more than seventy-five thousand volunteers into Union service, a higher per-capita contribution than any other state. The letters those soldiers wrote home, when they have survived, are kept across a scattered set of collections at the University of Iowa Libraries Special Collections, the State Historical Society of Iowa, and several regional historical societies.

This prototype digital edition presents a small curated sample of such letters, modeled with full descriptive metadata, presented with their transcriptions and digitized scans, and made searchable across creator, date, regiment, and subject. It is intended as a working illustration of what a faculty-partnered digital edition project might look like in its first delivered milestone.

A note on this prototype

The letters in this edition are illustrative entries built for demonstration. The names, regiments, dates, and Iowa geographic context reflect real Civil War history, but the transcribed content is fabricated for this portfolio piece. A production version of this edition would be transcribed, edited, and reviewed against actual primary-source holdings by Special Collections staff, with faculty editorial oversight. What's modeled here

  • Descriptive metadata structured on the Dublin Core Terms (DCMI) vocabulary, with a small set of local properties for regiment, company, and addressee. This kind of extension is what a faculty PI typically asks for early in a project.
  • IIIF-ready item detail pages, structured to consume IIIF Presentation API 3.0 manifests from a digitization workflow. The current prototype uses placeholder images and a public sample manifest.
  • Full-text search and faceted filters across all items.
  • WCAG 2.2 AA accessibility, including semantic HTML, keyboard navigation, focus indicators, skip-to-content link, descriptive alt text, and sufficient color contrast. Audited end-to-end with axe-core; zero violations across all pages.
  • A real Omeka S 4.1.1 backend running the same six items in a real Resource Template. It is described and linked on the methodology page.
  • A documented methodology page describing standards used, decisions made, and what production deployment would add on top.
